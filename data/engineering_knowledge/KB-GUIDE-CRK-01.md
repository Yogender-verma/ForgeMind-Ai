## Engineering Context & Failure Mechanisms
Surface cracks in machined metallic components typically originate from tensile mechanical stress concentrations exceeding local material yield strength. Common contributing factors in machining environments include:
1. **Tool Wear & Mechanical Shock:** Worn or chipped cutting tool edges drastically increase cutting friction and cyclic impact load.
2. **Clamping Fixture Pressure:** Non-uniform hydraulic or mechanical clamping loads impart localized shear gradients along workpiece boundaries.
3. **Thermal Quenching Gradients:** Rapid post-machining cooling fluctuations or uneven coolant delivery induce differential shrinkage micro-fractures.

## Advisory Investigation Steps
- Inspect fixture clamping alignment and verify hydraulic clamping pressure calibration.
- Review spindle cycle count and inspect tool cutting edge radius for flank wear.
- Verify coolant delivery nozzle flow rates to prevent thermal shock gradients.
